How I Automate a Two-Person Service Business With Free Tools

Published: November 23, 2026 · By Bryan Leonard, Qira LLC / Ah Crap Cleanup — Chandler, AZ

The goal: nothing lives only in my head

I run two small operations at once: Qira, a web and automation shop, and Ah Crap Cleanup, a pet-waste cleanup service in Chandler, Arizona. Both are essentially two-person operations — me and my operations manager. There is no office, no receptionist, and no budget for an all-in-one business platform. So the whole system is built from free tools I already had, wired together so that every lead, booking, and payment lands somewhere both of us can see it, without me retyping anything.

This is the exact stack I use today. It costs $0 beyond the tools themselves, and it works for any one- or two-person field service or freelance business.

1. One business number that forwards to email

Every customer contacts the same number: our Google Voice line. It is not my personal cell. Customers get one consistent number from the website, the Facebook page, and the yard signs — and Google Voice forwards every text to my email, so a message is never trapped on a phone I left in another room. It lands in the same inbox I check all day.

The practical effect: a customer can text at 9 PM, and I can read it, price it, and reply from anywhere. No missed-call voicemail black hole. The number is the front door, and the email forward is the backup.

2. A booking form that does the intake for me

For Ah Crap Cleanup, the website has a booking form built with Google Forms. It asks for the things I actually need to quote a job: name, service, address, yard details, and a photo upload. A customer who fills it out has already given me everything a five-minute phone call would have extracted — and the response lands in a spreadsheet I can sort, search, and share.

The same trick works for Qira: a free-audit form is the first step of every sales conversation. Instead of a discovery call that goes nowhere, the prospect answers a few questions about their business and what they need. By the time I reply, I already know whether the job is real and what to pitch. The form filters out the tire-kickers before they cost me an hour.

The rule: if I find myself asking the same five questions twice, those questions become a form.

3. One shared board for confirmed work only

The moment a customer agrees to a booking — a day, a time, a price — it goes on our shared ops board in Discord, in a channel the whole team can see. Name, service, agreed price, agreed time. Nothing else.

Quotes still waiting on a reply and general questions stay out of that channel; it holds confirmed work only, so anyone opening it sees the real schedule at a glance. That single habit is what keeps a two-person operation from double-booking. My operations manager sees the same list I see. If I am driving, asleep, or on a job, the day's work is still written down where the team can find it.

Discord is free, it works on every phone, and it beats a group text because the history is searchable and the channel structure keeps bookings separate from chatter.

4. Stripe for payments, no invoicing software

Payments run through Stripe. Customers pay by card through a payment link; there is no paper invoice, no "I'll Venmo you later," and no chasing a check. The money lands in the business account and the transaction record is the receipt — if a customer ever asks what they paid and when, the answer is one search away.

For recurring work, Stripe subscriptions handle the repeat billing automatically. A weekly cleanup client pays the same amount on the same schedule without either of us remembering to send anything. The only manual step is the first setup; after that, the billing runs itself.

5. Gmail filters as the triage desk

Everything funnels into one Gmail inbox: Google Voice forwards, form responses, Stripe receipts, customer replies. That would be chaos without filters. Form submissions get labeled automatically. Stripe receipts skip the inbox and go to a finance label. Anything from a known customer gets starred.

The inbox is the one place I check first every morning, and the filters mean the first screen I see is the work that needs a human — new leads and customer questions — not receipts and notifications. Fifteen minutes of filter setup saved me from hiring a virtual assistant to do the same sorting by hand.

What I deliberately did NOT buy

Every month a new all-in-one platform promises to replace this stack for $49 or $99 a month. I have not bought one, for three reasons. First, the free stack already does the job — a paid tool would have to save me real hours, not just look tidier. Second, each free tool does one thing well and can be swapped out without rebuilding everything; an all-in-one platform owns your data and your workflow. Third, at two people, the complexity budget matters more than the feature list: a system my operations manager and I both actually use beats a powerful system only I understand.

That may change as the business grows. When it does, the paid tool will have to earn its place against a specific, measured pain — not a sales page.

Start with the bottleneck, not the tool

If you run a small service business and want to automate, do not start by shopping for software. Start by finding the one place work gets lost: the text you forgot to answer, the quote that lived only in a phone call, the booking nobody wrote down. Fix that one leak with the simplest free tool that closes it. Then find the next leak.

That is how this stack was built — one leak at a time, over months, with tools I already had. It is not fancy. It just means no lead disappears, no booking gets double-sold, and no payment has to be chased. For a two-person business, that is the whole job.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need paid software to run a service business?
Not at two people. A business number, a form, a shared board, card payments, and inbox filters cover intake, booking, and payment. Buy software when a specific pain costs you more than the subscription.

Why Discord instead of a project management app?
Because both of us already have it on our phones, it is free, the history is searchable, and a channel that holds confirmed work only stays clean without any setup. Use whatever your team will actually open.

How do you keep leads from getting lost?
Every intake path — text, form, email — lands in one Gmail inbox, and every confirmed booking is copied to the shared board the same day. Nothing lives only in a text thread or in my head.
