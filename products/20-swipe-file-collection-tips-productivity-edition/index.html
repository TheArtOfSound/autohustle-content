20 practical swipe file items for productivity professionals.

1. The 1-3-5 Daily Method

Use case: Overwhelmed by daily tasks? Start small with this structured approach.

"Today, I will complete:

  • 1 big task (high impact)
  • 3 medium tasks (moderate effort)
  • 5 small tasks (quick wins)

Example:

  1. Big: Draft Q3 budget report
  2. Medium: Respond to client emails (3)
  3. Small: Organize desk, update calendar, review to-do list, hydrate, stretch"

2. The 2-Minute Rule

Use case: Prevent small tasks from piling up by handling them immediately.

"If a task takes less than 2 minutes, do it right now—no exceptions.

Example:

  • Reply to a Slack message: ✅
  • File a receipt: ✅
  • Delete a spam email: ✅"

3. The Eisenhower Matrix

Use case: Prioritize tasks based on urgency and importance to avoid reactive work.

"Categorize tasks into 4 quadrants:

  1. Urgent & Important (Do now)
  2. Not Urgent but Important (Schedule)
  3. Urgent but Not Important (Delegate)
  4. Not Urgent & Not Important (Eliminate)

Example:

  1. Submit quarterly taxes (Do now)
  2. Plan team training session (Schedule)
  3. Approve expense reports (Delegate)
  4. Scroll LinkedIn without purpose (Eliminate)"

4. Time Blocking Template

Use case: Regain control of your schedule by assigning fixed blocks of time to tasks.

"Example Schedule:

  • 9:00–10:30 AM: Deep work (Project X)
  • 10:30–10:45 AM: Break (walk)
  • 10:45–12:00 PM: Meetings (Client A, Team Sync)
  • 12:00–1:00 PM: Lunch (No screens)
  • 1:00–2:30 PM: Admin tasks (Emails, reports)
  • 2:30–3:00 PM: Buffer time (Overruns, urgent asks)"

5. The Pomodoro Technique

Use case: Beat procrastination with focused 25-minute sprints and short breaks.

"1. Choose a task

  1. Set a 25-minute timer
  2. Work until timer rings
  3. Take a 5-minute break
  4. Repeat 4x, then take a 15–30 minute break

Example:

  • Pomodoro 1: Outline blog post
  • Pomodoro 2: Write intro
  • Pomodoro 3: Draft first section
  • Pomodoro 4: Edit draft
  • Long break: Stretch, hydrate"

6. The “Two Lists” Rule

Use case: Reduce decision fatigue by limiting your daily focus to just two key tasks.

"Each morning, write down:

  • List A (Must Do): 1–2 critical tasks
  • List B (Nice to Do): Optional tasks

Example:

List A:

  1. Finalize client proposal
  2. Review Q3 KPIs

List B:

  • Update CRM
  • Brainstorm new project ideas"

7. The “Done List” Instead of To-Do List

Use case: Boost motivation by tracking what you’ve accomplished—not just what’s left.

"At the end of each day, log:

  • Tasks completed
  • Wins achieved
  • Lessons learned

Example:

✅ Submitted quarterly report

✅ Led team meeting (no interruptions)

✅ Automated weekly email template

🔍 Next time: Schedule fewer back-to-back meetings"

8. The “No-Meeting” Day

Use case: Protect deep work time by designating one day a week as meeting-free.

"Example: Every Wednesday is a ‘Focus Day’—no internal meetings, only client calls if time-sensitive.

Template for stakeholders:

‘To ensure uninterrupted work on [Project X], I’m reserving Wednesdays for deep work. Please send non-urgent requests by Tuesday or schedule for Thursday.’"

9. The “Email Triage” System

Use case: Process your inbox efficiently with a three-step approach.

"1. Delete/Archive: Trash or file irrelevant emails immediately.

  1. Delegate/Defer: Assign or schedule responses for 2+ minutes later.
  2. Do: Respond to emails that take <2 minutes now.

Example:

  • Newsletter: Archive
  • Client question: Defer to 3 PM
  • Quick approval needed: Do now"

10. The “Energy Audit”

Use case: Align high-energy tasks with your natural productivity peaks.

"Track energy levels for 3 days:

  • Morning (8–12 PM): High focus → Schedule analytical tasks
  • Afternoon (1–3 PM): Low energy → Do admin or creative work
  • Evening (4–6 PM): Moderate focus → Meetings or planning

Example:

8 AM: Write report

1 PM: Organize files

4 PM: Client check-in call"

11. The “80/20 Power Hour”

Use case: Focus on the 20% of activities that drive 80% of results.

"Each day, spend 60 minutes on your top 1–2 revenue-generating or high-impact tasks.

Example:

  • 9:00–10:00 AM: Work on client proposal (80% of potential revenue)
  • 10:00–10:30 AM: Follow up with high-value leads"

12. The “Batch Processing” Strategy

Use case: Save time by grouping similar tasks together.

"Dedicate specific blocks for:

  • Emails (e.g., 10–11 AM & 3–4 PM)
  • Meetings (e.g., Tues/Thurs afternoons)
  • Creative work (e.g., Monday mornings)

Example:

Every Monday 9–11 AM: Write all weekly blog posts

Every Wednesday 2–3 PM: Process invoices"

13. The “Pre-Commitment Device”

Use case: Force accountability by publicly committing to a deadline.

"Announce a hard deadline to your team or manager:

‘I will finalize the budget by Friday 5 PM—here’s the draft for review by Wednesday.’

Template:

‘I’m committing to deliver [X] by [date/time] to ensure [outcome]. I’ll send a status update by [earlier date].’"

14. The “Weekly Review Checklist”

Use case: Reflect and plan ahead to maintain long-term productivity.

"Use this checklist every Friday:

✅ Review goals for the week

✅ Update project statuses

✅ Archive old files

✅ Schedule next week’s top 3 tasks

✅ Send status update to manager

Example:

‘This week I completed X and Y, but Z was delayed. Next week, I’ll focus on A and B.’"

15. The “Parkinson’s Law” Hack

Use case: Combat procrastination by artificially limiting time for a task.

"Set a shorter deadline than you’d normally give yourself.

Example:

Instead of ‘Finish report by Friday,’ say:

‘I’ll finish the first draft by 2 PM today—no excuses.’"

16. The “Deep Work Contract”

Use case: Create a personal agreement to protect focus time.

"Write a one-sentence contract:

‘I commit to 90 minutes of uninterrupted deep work daily from 9–10:30 AM.’

Template:

‘Starting [date], I will dedicate [X hours] each day to [task] without distractions.’"

17. The “Decision Journal”

Use case: Improve future decisions by reviewing past choices.

"Log:

  • Decision made
  • Outcome
  • What you’d do differently

Example:

Decision: Declined Project B to prioritize Project A

Outcome: Project A succeeded; Project B client churned

Next time: Assess long-term client potential sooner"

18. The “Async Communication Guide”

Use case: Reduce unnecessary meetings with clear asynchronous communication norms.

"Set expectations:

  • Slack/email: Use for non-urgent updates
  • Calendar invites: Only for time-sensitive or collaborative discussions
  • Response time: 24 hours for emails, 4 hours for Slack

Template:

‘For quick questions, use Slack. For detailed discussions, book a 15-minute slot or reply by EOD.’"

19. The “Single-Tasking” Script”

Use case: Train your brain to focus on one thing at a time.

"When distracted, say:

‘I am currently working on [task]. I will return to [distraction] after I finish at [time].’

Example:

‘I’m writing the proposal now. I’ll check emails at 11:30 AM.’"

20. The “Weekly Wins” Report

Use case: End the week with momentum by celebrating progress.

"Send a short email to your team:

‘This week, I:

  • Completed [X]
  • Learned [Y]
  • Improved [Z]’

Template:

‘Hi team, here’s what I accomplished this week and what’s next:

✅ [Win 1]

✅ [Win 2]

🔜 Next week: [Goal]’"

6. The 2-Minute Rule for Instant Momentum

Use case: When you're feeling overwhelmed by small tasks that pile up.

Example text:

"Feeling stuck? Apply the 2-Minute Rule: if a task takes less than two minutes, do it immediately. This prevents minor tasks from cluttering your mental space and builds momentum for bigger projects. Try it now—reply to that quick email or file that document before moving to your next task."

7. Time Blocking for Laser Focus

Use case: When you need to eliminate distractions and hyperfocus on high-priority work.

Example text:

"Block your calendar for deep work sessions. For example:

  • 9:00–11:00 AM: Write quarterly report (no meetings, no emails).
  • 1:00–3:00 PM: Analyze Q2 data (phone on silent, browser tabs closed).

Stick to these blocks religiously. Use tools like Google Calendar or Outlook to enforce boundaries."

8. The Eisenhower Matrix for Task Prioritization

Use case: When you're juggling urgent and important tasks and need clarity.

Example text:

"Divide tasks into four quadrants:

  1. Urgent & Important (Do now): Client deadline, critical bug fix.
  2. Not Urgent but Important (Schedule): Long-term strategy, skill development.
  3. Urgent but Not Important (Delegate): Meeting requests, routine admin.
  4. Not Urgent & Not Important (Eliminate): Mindless scrolling, trivial busywork.

Example: Move 'Prepare client presentation' from quadrant 4 to 1—it’s both urgent and important."

9. The 1-3-5 Rule for Daily Wins

Use case: When you want to end each day with a sense of accomplishment.

Example text:

"Every morning, write down:

  • 1 big task (e.g., 'Launch new feature'),
  • 3 medium tasks (e.g., 'Draft user guide', 'Update FAQs', 'Schedule team sync'),
  • 5 small tasks (e.g., 'Reply to Slack messages', 'Review analytics', 'Back up files').

Complete the big task first—it sets the tone for the day."

10. The Pomodoro Technique for Sustained Focus

Use case: When you struggle with procrastination or mental fatigue.

Example text:

"Work in 25-minute sprints followed by a 5-minute break:

  1. Set a timer for 25 minutes.
  2. Focus solely on one task (e.g., coding, writing, designing).
  3. Take a 5-minute break—stand up, stretch, or hydrate.

After 4 Pomodoros, take a 15–30 minute break.

Example: Use TomatoTimer or a physical timer for accountability."

11. The "Two Lists" Method for Decision Fatigue

Use case: When you're paralyzed by too many choices or tasks.

Example text:

"Create two lists:

  • List A: Tasks you must do today (e.g., 'Submit expense report', 'Client call at 3 PM').
  • List B: Tasks you could do if time allows (e.g., 'Research new tools', 'Organize desk').

Stick to List A first. Only move to List B if List A is complete. This reduces overwhelm and ensures progress on what matters."

12. The "Eat the Frog" Strategy for Tackling Your Worst Task

Use case: When you procrastinate on the most challenging or dreaded task of the day.

Example text:

"Identify your 'frog' (the task you’re most likely to avoid). Do it first thing in the morning, before checking emails or Slack. Example:

  • Frog: 'Rewrite the project proposal by noon.'
  • Action: Block 8:00–10:00 AM with no interruptions. Tackle it head-on.

Pro tip: Pair this with the Pomodoro Technique for extra focus."

13. The "Not-to-Do" List for Eliminating Waste

Use case: When you’re bogged down by habits or tasks that drain time without adding value.

Example text:

"Write a 'Not-to-Do' list to eliminate time-wasters. Examples:

  • 'Don’t check email before 9 AM.'
  • 'Don’t attend meetings without a clear agenda.'
  • 'Don’t multitask during deep work sessions.'

Review this list weekly and adjust. Example: If replying to non-urgent Slack messages at night disrupts your sleep, add it to the list."

14. The "Parkinson’s Law" Hack to Beat Procrastination

Use case: When you waste time on low-priority tasks or allow tasks to expand to fill the time available.

Example text:

"Set artificial deadlines shorter than the actual deadline to force efficiency. Example:

  • Task: 'Write blog post' (due in 5 days).
  • Action: Schedule it for 2 days with a 30-minute Pomodoro timer.
  • Outcome: You’ll focus harder and finish faster. Use this for emails, reports, and even meetings (e.g., 'This meeting is 20 minutes, not 60')."

15. The "Batching" Technique for Efficiency

Use case: When you switch between too many small, repetitive tasks and lose momentum.

Example text:

"Group similar tasks and complete them in dedicated blocks. Examples:

  • Email batching: Check and respond to emails only at 10 AM and 4 PM.
  • Admin batching: Handle invoices, expense reports, and data entry in one 1-hour block on Fridays.
  • Creative batching: Write all social media posts for the week in one session.

Tools: Use apps like SaneBox for email batching or Toggl Track to time your batches."

16. The "5-Second Rule" to Overcome Hesitation

Use case: When you hesitate to start a task (e.g., going to the gym, starting a difficult project, or responding to a tough email).

Example text:

"Count down from 5 and physically move when you reach 1. Example:

  • Task: 'Start writing the case study.'
  • Action: '5-4-3-2-1—open the document and type the first sentence.'

This interrupts overthinking and builds momentum. Mel Robbins’ 5 Second Journal can help track progress."

17. The "Energy Audit" for Peak Productivity

Use case: When you’re exhausted but still not getting enough done.

Example text:

"Track your energy levels throughout the day for a week. Note:

  • Peak hours: When you feel most alert (e.g., 8–11 AM).
  • Slump hours: When you hit a wall (e.g., 2–4 PM).

Schedule high-focus tasks during peak hours and low-energy tasks (e.g., admin, emails) during slumps.

Example: If you’re a morning person, block 9–11 AM for deep work and save data entry for 3 PM."

18. The "Automate or Delegate" Workflow

Use case: When you’re stuck doing repetitive or low-value tasks that others could handle.

Example text:

"For any task, ask:

  1. Can this be automated? (e.g., email filters, Zapier workflows, scheduled social media posts).
  2. Can someone else do this? (e.g., hire a virtual assistant, delegate to a team member).

Example:

  • Automate: Use Zapier to auto-save email attachments to Google Drive.
  • Delegate: Outsource data entry to a freelancer on Upwork."

19. The "Weekly Review" Ritual for Continuous Improvement

Use case: When you want to reflect on progress, adjust priorities, and plan ahead.

Example text:

"Every Friday afternoon, spend 30–60 minutes reviewing:

  1. What went well? (e.g., 'Finished the quarterly report early').
  2. What didn’t? (e.g., 'Got distracted by non-urgent Slack messages').
  3. What’s next? (e.g., 'Schedule time for Q3 planning next week').

Use a template like the Weekly Review from GTD or Notion’s template."

20. The "Done List" for Motivation

Use case: When you feel like you didn’t accomplish enough, despite working hard.

Example text:

"Keep a 'Done List' to track daily wins. Examples:

  • 'Successfully migrated data to new CRM.'
  • 'Resolved 10 customer support tickets.'
  • 'Completed 3 Pomodoro sessions on the client project.'

Review it weekly to boost morale and identify patterns in your productivity.

Tools: Use a simple Google Doc, Notion, or Day One for journaling."
