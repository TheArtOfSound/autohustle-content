50 Professional Checklist Tips: Health Edition

Updated: April 01, 2026

50 practical checklist items for health professionals.

What's Inside

  • practical tips
  • ready to use
  • expert curated
  • [ ] Item 1: Verify patient identity using two identifiers (name, date of birth) before any procedure or treatment
  • [ ] Item 2: Document current medications, dosages, and allergies in patient history during initial assessment
  • [ ] Item 3: Assess vital signs (BP, HR, RR, SpO2, Temp) and record findings accurately
  • [ ] Item 4: Review past medical history for chronic conditions, surgeries, and hospitalizations
  • [ ] Item 5: Evaluate family history for hereditary diseases or genetic risks
  • [ ] Item 6: Perform a systems review (cardiac, respiratory, neurological, etc.) and document findings
  • [ ] Item 7: Conduct a focused physical exam based on presenting symptoms or chief complaint
  • [ ] Item 8: Screen for mental health concerns (mood, anxiety, cognition) using validated tools if indicated
  • [ ] Item 9: Assess pain level using a standardized scale (e.g., numeric rating, Wong-Baker FACES)
  • [ ] Item 10: Check immunization status and recommend updates if needed
  • [ ] Item 11: Order diagnostic tests (labs, imaging, cultures) based on clinical indications
  • [ ] Item 12: Confirm patient understanding of test preparation (e.g., fasting, hydration)
  • [ ] Item 13: Verify test results are reviewed and documented in a timely manner
  • [ ] Item 14: Compare current results with prior values to identify trends or changes
  • [ ] Item 15: Document any abnormalities and follow up with appropriate consultations
  • [ ] Item 16: Ensure proper labeling and chain of custody for all specimens
  • [ ] Item 17: Interpret imaging reports and correlate findings with clinical presentation
  • [ ] Item 18: Use point-of-care testing (e.g., glucometer, INR) only with quality controls
  • [ ] Item 19: Follow infection control protocols when collecting samples (hand hygiene, PPE)
  • [ ] Item 20: Confirm patient identity and match with test requisition before sample collection
  • [ ] Item 21: Establish clear, measurable treatment goals in collaboration with the patient
  • [ ] Item 22: Develop a personalized care plan addressing medical, psychological, and social needs
  • [ ] Item 23: Prioritize interventions based on urgency and evidence-based guidelines
  • [ ] Item 24: Discuss risks, benefits, and alternatives of proposed treatments with the patient
  • [ ] Item 25: Set follow-up intervals to monitor progress and adjust care as needed
  • [ ] Item 26: Coordinate care with specialists, therapists, or community resources when required
  • [ ] Item 27: Schedule procedures or interventions with attention to patient preferences and logistics
  • [ ] Item 28: Educate patients on self-management techniques for chronic conditions
  • [ ] Item 29: Involve family or caregivers in treatment planning if appropriate
  • [ ] Item 30: Use shared decision-making tools to align care with patient values
  • [ ] Item 31: Verify medication allergies prior to prescribing or administering any drug
  • [ ] Item 32: Confirm correct dosage, route, and frequency for prescribed medications
  • [ ] Item 33: Check for drug interactions using an updated clinical decision support system
  • [ ] Item 34: Educate patients on medication purpose, side effects, and adherence strategies
  • [ ] Item 35: Monitor for adverse drug reactions and document any observed effects
  • [ ] Item 36: Reconcile medications at every transition of care (admission, transfer, discharge)
  • [ ] Item 37: Use barcode scanning or electronic prescribing to reduce medication errors
  • [ ] Item 38: Provide clear instructions for over-the-counter medications and supplements
  • [ ] Item 39: Assess patient’s ability to self-administer medications (e.g., inhalers, injections)
  • [ ] Item 40: Document all medication changes with rationale and date of update
  • [ ] Item 41: Use plain language to explain diagnoses, tests, and treatments to patients
  • [ ] Item 42: Confirm patient understanding by asking them to repeat back key information
  • [ ] Item 43: Provide written materials or visual aids to support verbal instructions
  • [ ] Item 44: Encourage questions and address concerns without judgment
  • [ ] Item 45: Offer interpreter services for patients with limited English proficiency
  • [ ] Item 46: Discuss lifestyle modifications (diet, exercise, sleep) tailored to patient needs
  • [ ] Item 47: Provide resources for community support (e.g., smoking cessation programs)
  • [ ] Item 48: Teach warning signs of complications and when to seek urgent care
  • [ ] Item 49: Follow up with patients to assess comprehension and adherence
  • [ ] Item 50: Document all patient education provided and their response to it
