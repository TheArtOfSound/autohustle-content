Template 1: Core Remote Work Agreement

When to use: As a foundational document to outline the basic expectations and responsibilities for remote work within your small team.

Remote Work Agreement

Effective Date: [DATE]

This Remote Work Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into by and between [COMPANY NAME] ("Company") and [EMPLOYEE NAME] ("Employee").

  1. Purpose

The purpose of this Agreement is to establish the terms and conditions under which Employee may work remotely from a location other than the Company’s primary office.

  1. Remote Work Eligibility
  • Remote work is available to [JOB TITLE/ROLE] positions.
  • Employee must have completed [X] months of service with the Company before being eligible for remote work.
  • The Company reserves the right to approve or deny remote work requests based on business needs.
  1. Work Location
  • Employee’s remote work location must be in [COUNTRY/STATE/PROVINCE] unless otherwise approved by the Company.
  • Employee must ensure a suitable and safe work environment, free from distractions and hazards.
  • Any change in the remote work location must be approved in writing by the Company.
  1. Work Hours and Availability
  • Employee is expected to maintain the same core work hours as in-office employees: [START TIME] to [END TIME], [TIME ZONE].
  • Employee must be available for meetings and collaboration during core hours unless otherwise agreed upon.
  • Employee must use [SLACK/TEAMS/OTHER] for real-time communication and [EMAIL/ASYNC TOOL] for non-urgent matters.
  1. Equipment and Resources
  • The Company will provide: [LIST OF EQUIPMENT, E.G., LAPTOP, MONITOR, HEADSET].
  • Employee is responsible for ensuring all provided equipment is used solely for work purposes and is kept in good working condition.
  • Employee must report any equipment issues to [IT CONTACT] immediately.
  1. Data Security and Confidentiality
  • Employee must adhere to the Company’s [DATA SECURITY POLICY NAME] and all applicable data protection laws.
  • Employee must use [VPN/SECURE CONNECTION] when accessing Company systems remotely.
  • All work-related files must be stored in approved Company systems (e.g., [GOOGLE DRIVE/DROPBOX/ETC.]).
  1. Performance and Productivity
  • Employee’s performance will be evaluated based on [OUTPUT/GOALS/KEY RESULTS] rather than hours logged.
  • Employee must participate in regular check-ins with their manager: [FREQUENCY, E.G., WEEKLY 1:1s, MONTHLY GOAL REVIEWS].
  • Employee must track and report progress on tasks using [PROJECT MANAGEMENT TOOL, E.G., ASANA/TRELLO].
  1. Communication Guidelines
  • Urgent matters should be communicated via [CHANNEL, E.G., SLACK DIRECT MESSAGE/PHONE CALL].
  • Non-urgent matters can be communicated via [CHANNEL, E.G., EMAIL/ASYNC COMMENTS].
  • Employee must respond to messages within [X] hours during core business hours.
  1. Expenses and Reimbursements
  • The Company will reimburse reasonable expenses incurred for remote work, up to $[AMOUNT] per month. Receipts must be submitted via [EXPENSE TRACKING SYSTEM].
  • Approved expenses include: [LIST, E.G., INTERNET, HOME OFFICE SUPPLIES].

10. Compliance with Company Policies

  • Employee must comply with all Company policies, including but not limited to [CODE OF CONDUCT, ANTI-HARASSMENT POLICY, ETC.].
  • Violations of this Agreement or Company policies may result in termination of remote work privileges or disciplinary action.

11. Termination of Remote Work Arrangement

  • Either party may terminate this Agreement with [X] days’ written notice.
  • The Company may terminate this Agreement at any time for business reasons or if Employee fails to meet performance expectations.

12. Miscellaneous

  • This Agreement supersedes all prior agreements related to remote work.
  • Any modifications to this Agreement must be in writing and signed by both parties.
  • This Agreement is governed by the laws of [STATE/COUNTRY].

Employee Acknowledgment:

I, [EMPLOYEE NAME], have read and agree to the terms of this Remote Work Agreement.

Signature: ________________________ Date: [DATE]

Manager Acknowledgment:

I, [MANAGER NAME], have discussed this Remote Work Agreement with [EMPLOYEE NAME] and confirm their eligibility for remote work.

Signature: ________________________ Date: [DATE]

Template 2: Communication Expectations Policy

When to use: To clearly define how your team should communicate while working remotely, ensuring transparency and efficiency.

Communication Expectations Policy

Purpose

This policy establishes guidelines for communication among team members working remotely. Clear communication is essential for collaboration, productivity, and maintaining team cohesion.

1. Communication Channels

  • Primary Channel: [SLACK/TEAMS/DISCORD] for real-time communication.
  • Secondary Channel: [EMAIL/ASYNC TOOL, E.G., LOOM/LOOMY] for non-urgent matters.
  • Emergency Contact: [PHONE NUMBER/ON-CALL SYSTEM] for urgent issues outside core hours.

2. Response Times

  • Urgent Matters (P0/P1): Respond within [X] hours, 24/7/365.
  • High Priority (P2): Respond within [X] hours during core business hours [TIME RANGE].
  • Standard Priority (P3): Respond within [X] business days.
  • Low Priority (P4): No SLA; respond as time permits.

3. Meeting Guidelines

  • Scheduling: All meetings must be scheduled in [CALENDLY/GOOGLE CALENDAR/OUTLOOK] with at least [X] hours’ notice.
  • Agenda: Every meeting must have a clear agenda shared [X] hours in advance.
  • Recording: Encourage recording meetings for team members in different time zones or with conflicting schedules. Recordings should be stored in [DRIVE FOLDER].
  • Attendance: Mandatory for [ROLES/TEAMS]. Optional for others; notify organizer if unable to attend.

4. Async Communication Best Practices

  • Updates: Share daily/weekly updates in [TEAM CHANNEL/PROJECT TRACKER]. Include:
  • Tasks completed.
  • Tasks in progress.
  • Blockers or assistance needed.
  • Documentation: All decisions, discussions, and key information must be documented in [CONFLUENCE/NOTION/GOOGLE DOCS].
  • Threaded Discussions: Use threads in [SLACK/TEAMS] to keep conversations organized.

5. Availability and Status

  • Status Updates: Use [STATUS FEATURES, E.G., SLACK STATUS, TEAMS ACTIVITY STATUS] to indicate availability:
  • Available: Online and responsive.
  • Away: Briefly unavailable (e.g., lunch, short break).
  • Focus Time: Do not disturb; will respond later.
  • Offline: Not working (e.g., after hours, weekends).
  • Core Hours: Employees are expected to be available for synchronous communication during [TIME RANGE, E.G., 10 AM - 3 PM EST].

6. Escalation Procedures

  • Blockers: If a task is blocked for more than [X] hours, escalate to [MANAGER/TEAM LEAD] via [CHANNEL].
  • Conflicts: For interpersonal conflicts, follow the [CONFLICT RESOLUTION POLICY].

7. Language and Tone

  • Be clear, concise, and professional in all written communication.
  • Avoid sarcasm or jokes in written form to prevent misunderstandings.
  • Use emojis sparingly and appropriately to convey tone.

8. Compliance

  • Failure to adhere to these guidelines may result in [DISCIPLINARY ACTION, E.G., WRITTEN WARNING, LOSS OF REMOTE PRIVILEGES].

Template 3: Availability and Response Policy

When to use: To set clear expectations around when team members are expected to be available and how quickly they should respond to messages.

Availability and Response Policy

Purpose

This policy ensures that team members are aligned on availability expectations and response times, reducing uncertainty and improving collaboration.

1. Core Hours

  • Core hours are [START TIME] to [END TIME] in [TIME ZONE].
  • During core hours, all team members must be available for real-time communication and collaboration.
  • Exceptions must be approved by [MANAGER/HR] in advance.

2. Flexible Hours Policy

  • Outside of core hours, team members may work flexible hours to accommodate personal schedules.
  • Flexible hours are not a requirement but an option for those who need them.
  • Team members are not expected to respond to messages outside their scheduled work hours unless it is an emergency.

3. Response Time Expectations

| Priority Level | Response Time | Channel |

|----------------|---------------|------------------|

| P0 - Critical | Within 30 min | Phone/Slack DM |

| P1 - High | Within 2 hours| Slack/Teams |

| P2 - Medium | Within 24 hrs | Slack/Email |

| P3 - Low | Within 48 hrs | Email/Async Tool |

4. Vacation and Time Off

  • Team members must update their [CALENDLY/GOOGLE CAALNDAR/OUTLOOK] status to reflect time off.
  • Out-of-office (OOO) messages must include:
  • Dates of absence.
  • An alternative contact for urgent matters.
  • Expected return date.
  • For extended absences (3+ days), team members must provide a project update to their manager before leaving.

5. Meeting-Free Blocks

  • Team members can request [X] hours per week of meeting-free time to focus on deep work.
  • Requests must be submitted to [MANAGER/TEAM] at least [X] days in advance.
  • Approved blocks will be reflected in team calendars.

6. On-Call Expectations

  • On-call duties rotate weekly among [TEAM/ROLE].
  • On-call team members must:
  • Be available via phone during their shift.
  • Respond to alerts within [X] minutes.
  • Document all incidents in [INCIDENT LOG TOOL].
  • Compensation for on-call duties: [DESCRIBE, E.G., 1.5x hourly rate, extra PTO].

7. Emergency Procedures

  • In case of a true emergency (e.g., system outage, security breach), team members must:
  • Notify [EMERGENCY CONTACT] immediately via phone.
  • Follow the [EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROTOCOL].
  • Document the incident afterward in [INCIDENT REPORTING SYSTEM].

8. Compliance and Accountability

  • Managers will review response times and availability monthly to ensure compliance.
  • Persistent failure to meet response time expectations may result in [DISCIPLINARY ACTION, E.g., written warning, loss of flexible hours].

Template 4: Performance and Productivity Guidelines

When to use: To shift the focus from hours worked to measurable outcomes, ensuring accountability and clarity for remote teams.

Performance and Productivity Guidelines

Purpose

This policy outlines how performance and productivity will be measured for remote team members, emphasizing outcomes over hours logged.

1. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Each role will have [X] primary KPIs that align with company goals. Examples include:

  • Sales Team: Number of deals closed, revenue generated, lead conversion rate.
  • Marketing Team: Campaign engagement, lead generation, brand awareness metrics.
  • Engineering Team: Features shipped, bug resolution rate, code review turnaround.
  • Customer Support: Ticket resolution time, customer satisfaction (CSAT) score, first-contact resolution rate.

2. Goal-Setting and Tracking

  • Goals will be set [FREQUENCY, E.G., quarterly/bi-weekly] using the [OKR/OKR/OTHER] framework.
  • Goals must be:
  • Specific: Clearly defined.
  • Measurable: Quantifiable where possible.
  • Achievable: Realistic given resources.
  • Relevant: Aligned with team/company objectives.
  • Time-bound: Deadlines included.
  • Goals will be tracked in [GOAL TRACKING TOOL, E.G., ASANA, NOTION, JIRA].

3. Progress Reviews

  • Weekly Check-ins: Short updates shared in [TEAM CHANNEL/PROJECT TRACKER]. Include:
  • Progress on current goals.
  • Upcoming tasks.
  • Blockers or assistance needed.
  • Bi-Weekly 1:1s: Scheduled meetings with direct managers to discuss:
  • Progress toward goals.
  • Challenges or obstacles.
  • Career development and support needed.
  • Monthly Reviews: Formal review of KPIs and goal progress. Metrics will be compared against targets.

4. Productivity Metrics

While hours logged are not the primary measure of productivity, the following metrics may be tracked:

  • Task Completion Rate: Percentage of assigned tasks completed on time.
  • Time to Completion: Average time taken to complete tasks.
  • Quality Metrics: Error rate, customer feedback scores, peer reviews.
  • Collaboration Metrics: Frequency of updates shared, participation in team discussions.

5. Addressing Underperformance

  • If a team member consistently underperforms against KPIs or productivity metrics, the following steps will be taken:
  • Informal Feedback: Manager provides specific, actionable feedback.
  • Performance Improvement Plan (PIP): Formal plan outlining expectations, timeline ([X] weeks), and support provided.
  • Consequences: Failure to improve may result in [LOSS OF REMOTE PRIVILEGES, TERMINATION, ETC.].

6. Recognition and Rewards

  • High performers will be recognized through:
  • Public praise in [TEAM MEETINGS/COMPANY WIDE COMMUNICATIONS].
  • Bonuses or incentives tied to performance (e.g., [X]% of salary for exceeding targets).
  • Opportunities for professional development (e.g., conferences, training).

7. Tools and Resources

  • Project Management: [ASANA/TRELLO/JIRA] for tracking tasks and projects.
  • Time Tracking (Optional): [TOGGL/HARVEST] for teams that benefit from time tracking.
  • Productivity Analytics: [RESCUE TIME/TEMPO] for optional self-tracking.
  • Communication: [SLACK/TEAMS] for real-time updates.

8. Transparency and Trust

  • Managers will provide regular feedback and support to help team members succeed.
  • Team members are encouraged to proactively communicate challenges or needs.
  • The Company trusts team members to manage their time effectively to meet goals.

2. Employee Responsibilities

  • Employees are responsible for:
  • Using provided equipment solely for work purposes.
  • Keeping equipment in good working condition and reporting any issues immediately to [IT CONTACT].
  • Following Company guidelines for [DATA SECURITY/DEVICE USAGE].
  • Returning equipment in good condition if remote work privileges are revoked or employment ends.

3. Home Office Setup Guidelines

To ensure a safe and productive work environment, employees should:

  • Ergonomics:
  • Use a chair that supports good posture.

  • Use an external keyboard and mouse to maintain a neutral wrist position.
  • Take regular breaks to stretch and move around.
  • Lighting:
  • Ensure the workspace is well-lit to reduce eye strain.
  • Avoid glare on screens by positioning monitors away from windows or using anti-glare screens.
  • Internet Connection:
  • Maintain a stable internet connection with a minimum speed of [X] Mbps download/[X] Mbps upload.
  • Use a wired connection if possible for stability.
  • Noise Levels:
  • Minimize background noise during meetings and focused work time.
  • Use noise-canceling headphones if needed.

  • Furniture: Ergonomic chair, desk, monitor stand.

  • Internet Upgrades: Costs to upgrade internet speed (up to $[AMOUNT]).
  • Office Decor: Plants, lighting, artwork (up to $[AMOUNT]).

  3. Reimbursements will be processed within [X] business days.

Last Updated: [DATE]

This framework ensures fair and consistent performance evaluations for employees working remotely. It aligns with company goals while accounting for the unique challenges of remote work.

Employees will be evaluated on the following metrics, tailored to their role:

For All Employees:

  • Productivity: Completion of tasks within deadlines.
  • Quality of Work: Accuracy, thoroughness, and adherence to company standards.

Role-Specific KPIs:

Self-Assessment:

Template 15: Remote Work Security & Data Protection Policy

When to use: To ensure employees understand their responsibilities for protecting company data while working remotely.

Remote Work Security & Data Protection Policy

Effective Date: [DATE]

Last Updated: [DATE]

#### 1. Purpose

This policy establishes guidelines to protect company data, intellectual property, and client information when employees work remotely. Compliance is mandatory to mitigate risks such as data breaches, unauthorized access, or loss of sensitive information.

#### 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All employees, contractors, and third parties with access to company data.
  • All devices (company-owned or personal) used to access company systems.

#### 3. Data Classification

Company data is classified as follows:

| Classification | Definition | Examples | Access Level |

|--------------------|----------------|--------------|------------------|

| Public | No restrictions on sharing. | Company blog posts, marketing materials. | Open access. |

| Internal | For employee use only. | Internal memos, project plans. | Limited to company employees. |

| Confidential | Highly sensitive; restricted access. | Financial reports, employee records. | Approved employees only. |

| Restricted | Critical to business operations; highest protection. | Source code, client contracts, PII (Personally Identifiable Information). | Strict access controls. |

#### 4. Device Security

Company-Owned Devices:

  • Must have [full-disk encryption, e.g., BitLocker or FileVault] enabled.
  • Must run up-to-date antivirus/anti-malware software (e.g., CrowdStrike, Malwarebytes).
  • Must use a [VPN, e.g., NordVPN or Cisco AnyConnect] when accessing company networks.
  • Must lock the device when unattended (Windows: Win + L; Mac: Ctrl + Cmd + Q).
  • Must not be shared with family or friends.

Personal Devices:

  • Employees may use personal devices for work only with prior approval from [IT/Manager].
  • Personal devices must meet the same security standards as company devices (e.g., encryption, updated software).
  • Employees must install company-mandated security tools (e.g., antivirus, VPN).

#### 5. Network Security

  • Employees must use a secure, password-protected Wi-Fi network. Public Wi-Fi (e.g., coffee shops, airports) is prohibited for work unless connected via VPN.
  • Home Wi-Fi routers must use [WPA3 encryption] and a strong, unique password.
  • Employees must not use unauthorized cloud storage (e.g., personal Google Drive, Dropbox) to store company data.

#### 6. Password & Authentication

  • All accounts must use strong, unique passwords (minimum 12 characters, including upper/lowercase, numbers, and symbols).
  • Employees must enable [Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)] for all company accounts.
  • Passwords must be changed every [X] days.
  • Passwords must not be shared or written down in unsecured locations.

#### 7. Data Handling & Storage

  • Do:
  • Store company data only on approved platforms (e.g., [Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, SharePoint]).
  • Use company-approved file-sharing tools (e.g., [Slack, Microsoft Teams, Notion]).
  • Back up critical data regularly to company-approved cloud storage.
  • Do Not:
  • Store company data on personal devices or external drives (e.g., USB sticks) unless encrypted and approved.
  • Use unauthorized apps (e.g., personal email, messaging apps like WhatsApp) for work communications.
  • Print or download sensitive documents to personal printers or devices.

#### 8. Phishing & Social Engineering

  • Employees must be vigilant for phishing attempts (e.g., suspicious emails, links, or attachments).
  • Never click on links or download attachments from unknown senders.
  • Report suspicious activity immediately to [IT Security Team/Email].
  • Verify unusual requests (e.g., wire transfers, password resets) via a secondary channel (e.g., phone call).

#### 9. Remote Work Environment

  • Workspaces should be free from distractions and secure from family members/pets.
  • Employees must ensure no one is observing their screen during sensitive work.
  • Screens should be locked when stepping away from the workspace.
  • Avoid working in public spaces (e.g., cafes, co-working spaces) unless absolutely necessary.

#### 10. Incident Reporting

Any suspected or actual security incidents (e.g., data breach, lost device, unauthorized access) must be reported immediately to [IT Security Team/Email].

Incident Response Steps:

  1. Isolate the affected device (if possible).
  2. Do not attempt to fix the issue yourself.
  3. Provide details (e.g., time, nature of incident, affected data) to IT.
  4. Cooperate fully with any investigations.

#### 11. BYOD (Bring Your Own Device) Policy

Employees using personal devices for work must comply with:

  • Installing all required security software.
  • Allowing remote wiping of company data if the device is lost, stolen, or compromised.
  • Granting IT access for security audits if requested.
  • Returning all company data upon termination or role change.

#### 12. Training & Awareness

  • Employees must complete [annual/quarterly] security training.
  • IT will provide regular updates on new threats and best practices.
  • Employees must acknowledge receipt of this policy annually.

#### 13. Policy Violations

Violations of this policy may result in:

  • Disciplinary action, up to and including termination.
  • Legal action if a violation leads to a data breach or legal liability.

#### 14. Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed [annually/bi-annually] or as laws/technology change.

Contact: For questions or to report incidents, contact [IT Security Team Email/Phone].

Acknowledgment:

I, [Employee Name], have read and understood the Remote Work Security & Data Protection Policy. I agree to comply with all its terms.
