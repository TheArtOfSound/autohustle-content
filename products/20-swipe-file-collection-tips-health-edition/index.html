1. Quick Nutrition Assessment Questions

Use case: First patient consultation or annual check-up

Example Text:

*"To better understand your daily nutrition, I’ll ask a few quick questions. In the past week, how often did you:

  • Eat at least 5 servings of fruits/vegetables?
  • Consume sugary drinks or snacks?
  • Skip breakfast or other meals?
  • Include protein with each meal?

This helps identify small, actionable changes to improve your diet."*

2. Hydration Reminder Script for Clinicians

Use case: During patient education or follow-up visits

Example Text:

"Did you know that even mild dehydration can cause fatigue, headaches, or poor concentration? Let’s set a simple goal: aim for half your body weight (lbs) in ounces daily. For example, if you weigh 150 lbs, try to drink 75 oz of water. Start with a glass upon waking, and carry a reusable bottle to track intake."

3. Sample 5-Minute Desk Stretch Routine

Use case: For patients with sedentary jobs or chronic pain

Example Text:

*"Try this quick routine every hour if you’re at a desk:

  1. Seated Twist: Rotate gently left/right, holding each side for 5 seconds.
  2. Neck Rolls: Slow circles to release tension.
  3. Shoulder Shrugs: Lift shoulders, hold, then release.
  4. Wrist Stretches: Extend arms, pull fingers back gently.
  5. Stand & Reach: Arms overhead, stretch upward. Repeat 3x."*

4. Stress-Reduction Breathing Technique

Use case: For patients with anxiety, hypertension, or insomnia

Example Text:

*"The 4-7-8 Breath is simple yet powerful:

  1. Inhale quietly through your nose for 4 seconds.
  2. Hold your breath for 7 seconds.
  3. Exhale completely through your mouth (with a whoosh sound) for 8 seconds.

Repeat 4x daily or whenever stressed. It slows the heart rate and promotes relaxation."*

5. Sleep Hygiene Checklist for Patients

Use case: Insomnia or poor sleep quality management

Example Text:

*"Follow this nightly routine:

☑ Keep a consistent sleep/wake time (even weekends).

☑ Avoid screens 1 hour before bed (blue light disrupts sleep).

☑ Limit caffeine after 2 PM and alcohol before bed.

☑ Optimize your room: cool, dark, and quiet.

☑ Wind down with a book or meditation—no work in bed!

Track your sleep for 2 weeks and share patterns with me."*

6. Motivational Interviewing Phrases for Behavior Change

Use case: Discussing lifestyle modifications with reluctant patients

Example Text:

"Instead of: ‘You should* exercise more.’ Try:

  • ‘What might make walking 3x/week feel manageable for you?’
  • ‘On a scale of 1–10, how ready are you to try this?’
  • ‘What’s one small step you’ve succeeded with before?’

Goal: Elicit their own reasons for change, not impose yours."*

7. Chronic Disease Prevention Script

Use case: Educating patients about diabetes, heart disease, or hypertension

Example Text:

*"Three pillars to prevent chronic disease:

  1. Move daily: Even 10-minute walks lower blood sugar and blood pressure.
  2. Eat whole foods: Focus on fiber, lean proteins, and healthy fats (avocados, nuts, olive oil).
  3. Monitor trends: Track your numbers (BP, glucose) at home and share with me.

Small, consistent changes > occasional extremes."*

8. Postpartum Depression Screening Script

Use case: Well-woman visits or pediatric follow-ups

Example Text:

*"After giving birth, it’s normal to feel exhausted or overwhelmed. But if you’ve had persistent sadness, hopelessness, or disinterest in your baby for over 2 weeks, let’s talk. I ask every mom:

  • ‘How have you been feeling emotionally since delivery?’
  • ‘Do you feel like you’re bonding with your baby?’

Screening tools like the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale can help."*

9. Testicular Self-Exam Guide for Patients

Use case: Men’s health discussions or urology referrals

Example Text:

*"Perform this monthly:

  1. Timing: After a warm shower (scrotum is relaxed).
  2. Steps:
  • Hold each testicle gently between thumb/ fingers.
  • Roll it slowly to feel for lumps, changes in size, or pain.
  • Note any heaviness or dull ache.

Lumps aren’t always cancerous, but report anything unusual to your doctor immediately."

10. Pediatric Fever Management Protocol

Use case: Parent education for fever in children

Example Text:

*"For fever (temp >100.4°F):

  1. Comfort first: Hydration, lightweight clothing, and cool room.
  2. Medication:
  • Infants <6 months: Call doctor before meds.
  • Older kids: Acetaminophen or ibuprofen (per weight guidelines).
  1. Seek care if:
  • Fever >104°F or lasts >72 hours.
  • Child is lethargic, not drinking, or has rash/spots.

Never give aspirin to kids (Reye’s syndrome risk)."

11. Fall Prevention Home Safety Tips

Use case: Geriatric care or caregiver education

Example Text:

*"Reduce fall risks at home:

☑ Remove throw rugs or secure them with non-slip pads.

☑ Install grab bars in bathrooms (near toilet/shower).

☑ Ensure hallways/stairs are well-lit (nightlights for bathroom trips).

☑ Encourage sturdy shoes (no slippers or flip-flops).

☑ Schedule a home safety audit with an occupational therapist."*

13. EHR Templates for Pediatric Growth Tracking

Use case: Well-child visits or developmental screenings

Example Text:

*"In your EHR, add these fields:

  • Growth Percentiles: Height/weight/BMI plotted on CDC charts.
  • Developmental Milestones: Check off (e.g., ‘Walks alone’ at 12 months).
  • Vaccination Status: Auto-flag missing doses.
  • Parent Concerns: Free-text section for caregiver notes.

Tip: Use smart phrases to auto-populate common findings (e.g., ‘No concerns noted for [age]-month-old.’)."*

14. Evidence-Based Pain Management Alternatives

Use case: Discussing non-opioid options with patients

Example Text:

*"For chronic pain, consider:

  • Physical therapy: Strengthening/stretching reduces joint strain.
  • CBT: Helps reframe pain perceptions.
  • Mindfulness: Apps like Headspace or Calm guide meditation.
  • Topical treatments: Lidocaine patches or capsaicin cream.

Always assess pain scales (0–10) and functional goals (e.g., ‘Walk 30 mins daily’)."

15. Patient Education Handout: Heart-Healthy Grocery List

Use case: Nutrition counseling for cardiovascular health

Example Text:

*"Fill your cart with:

✅ Produce: Spinach, berries, avocados, sweet potatoes.

✅ Proteins: Salmon, lentils, skinless poultry, tofu.

✅ Grains: Quinoa, oats, brown rice.

✅ Fats: Olive oil, almonds, chia seeds.

❌ Avoid: Processed meats, sugary cereals, frozen meals with >400mg sodium.

Pro tip: Shop the perimeter of the store—fresh foods are usually there!"*

16. Self-Care Prescription for Clinicians

Use case: Burnout prevention for healthcare providers

Example Text:

*"Fill your own prescription:

  • Daily: 10 mins of silence (no phone/email).
  • Weekly: ‘No-work’ hobby (gardening, painting).
  • Monthly: Peer support group (vent + share wins).
  • Annually: CME on resilience (e.g., Schwartz Rounds).

Reminder: You can’t pour from an empty cup. Prioritize your well-being."*

17. Health Policy Talking Points for Advocacy

Use case: Discussions with policymakers or community leaders

Example Text:

*"Key messages for supporting public health:

  1. Prevention saves costs: Every $1 spent on childhood vaccines saves $10 in healthcare.
  2. Health equity: Expand access to primary care in underserved areas.
  3. Mental health: Integrate behavioral health into primary care (collaborative care models).
  4. Data privacy: Balance innovation (telehealth) with patient confidentiality.

Example: ‘We urge you to invest in [Policy X] to reduce [Disease Y] in our community.’"*

18. Emerging Trend: Wearable Tech for Chronic Conditions

Use case: Patient education on digital health tools

Example Text:

*"Wearables like Apple Watch ECG or Abbott’s Freestyle Libre (glucose monitor) empower patients:

  • Real-time alerts: Irregular heart rhythms or glucose spikes.
  • Data sharing: Syncs with EHRs for clinician review.
  • Motivation: Step goals or medication reminders.

Caution: Ensure devices are FDA-cleared and FDA-approved for your patient’s condition."*

19. Cultural Competency Phrases for Patient Interviews

Use case: Cross-cultural healthcare communication

Example Text:

*"Adapt your language to show respect:

  • Hispanic/Latinx patients: ‘¿Qué le preocupa más sobre su salud?’ (What concerns you most about your health?)
  • Asian patients: ‘¿Hay algo en su dieta o rutina que le gustaría cambiar?’ (Is there anything in your diet/routine you’d like to change?)
  • Middle Eastern patients: ‘¿Cómo podemos adaptar el plan a sus creencias?’ (How can we adapt the plan to your beliefs?)

Avoid assumptions—ask open-ended questions."

20. Quick Guide to Interpreting Lab Results

Use case: Patient education during follow-ups

Example Text:

*"When reviewing labs, highlight:

  • Normal ranges: Always compare to your personal baseline (not just lab averages).
  • Trends: ‘Your A1C went from 8.0 to 6.8—great improvement!’
  • Actionable items: ‘Your vitamin D is low; let’s add a supplement.’
  • Red flags: ‘Your LDL is 190—we’ll discuss statins and diet.’

Tip: Use analogies: ‘Your kidneys filter waste like a coffee filter—we want it clean and clear!’"
