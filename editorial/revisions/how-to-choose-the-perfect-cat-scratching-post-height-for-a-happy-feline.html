Choose a cat scratching post by observing your cat

Revised: November 27, 2026

Start with observation rather than a product ranking. Notice where your cat already scratches: a vertical sofa corner, a flat rug, a cardboard box or a textured mat. Those habits help you choose a surface that is more likely to be used.

Match the position and texture

Vertical posts, horizontal pads and inclined scratchers offer different positions. If your cat prefers a vertical surface, look for enough usable height for its natural stretching posture. If it scratches rugs, consider a horizontal option. Texture preferences differ; an inexpensive suitable pad may be more useful than a large tower the cat ignores.

Check stability before appearance

Review dimensions, construction and anchoring instructions. A scratching surface should not unexpectedly tip or shift during intended use. Follow installation instructions and inspect exposed fasteners, loose material and damaged sections. If choosing a wall-mounted design, use the mounting method specified for the wall and product.

Put it where the behavior already happens

Consider placing the new scratching surface near an existing scratching location or a resting area. Keep it accessible and avoid blocking paths. Let the cat investigate at its own pace instead of forcing its paws onto the surface.

Run a simple observation trial

Keep the location consistent for several days and note whether your cat approaches and uses it. If it is ignored, try changing the position or offering another suitable texture rather than repeatedly buying the same style. Inspect the surface regularly and replace worn or damaged parts as needed.

What to look for at purchase

  • Dimensions that fit the available space and your cat's posture.
  • Clear installation and care instructions.
  • Materials and construction appropriate for intended use.
  • Replacement options and a clear return policy.

This guide is about equipment selection, not veterinary diagnosis or treatment. A sudden change in behavior, mobility or claw condition warrants advice from a veterinarian.
